micro.breaks

Move a little, every hour you sit.

Problem

Office workers spend most of the workday seated, in long unbroken blocks. An objective study of 231 office workers found they sat for 79% of work hours, and 42% of work hours in bouts of 30 min or more. Nothing in the workday cues them to stop. By the time they notice, the cost has landed: a stiff neck or lower back, an afternoon energy crash, focus that fades before the work is done. Low back pain is the single leading cause of disability worldwide, affecting 619 million people in 2020.

The fix is small and proven. Five minutes of walking every 30 min cut blood-sugar spikes by 58%, lowered blood pressure by 4–5 mmHg, and reduced fatigue compared with sitting all day. Yet existing break reminders don't stick: they fire mid-call or mid-flow, get snoozed, then switched off. When people do stop, they don't know what to do with two minutes, especially in an open space. And nothing shows whether they are actually moving more.

Who it's for

  • Desk-bound office workers on a laptop 6–8 h a day, mostly in Chrome, working from home and from an open-space office.
  • V1 user: the author. One test week, one person, to prove the habit forms before anyone else uses it.

Goal

Success after the test week (5 working days).

  • ≤ 60 minLongest seated streak, every working day.
  • ≥ 80%of prompts end in an actual break. Prompts cut short by lunch don't count.
  • ≥ 1break started from the prompt every working day.
  • YesOn Friday, I want to keep using it next week.

Solution

A Chrome extension that starts on its own when you're active on your computer during your hours, as long as Chrome is open. A battery on every new tab drains while you sit. Five minutes before it runs out, a heads-up; at zero, micro.breaks opens a new tab and switches you to it. You pick a simple break, a timer starts, and you step away from the computer for at least 5 minutes. When you come back, the battery is recharged.

Active in Chrome Battery drains Heads-up at −5 min "Time to move." 5+ min away from the computer "Recharged."

Three milestones: M1 the test-week extension, local only · M2 exercise library, accounts, sync, Google Calendar and Outlook · M3 sharing and teams.

Product rules

M1 · defaults, editable on the movement timer
Movement timer (settings)
Interval
Remind me to move every 60 min. Options: 30, 45, 60, 75, 90 min.
Working hours
9:00–18:00, Mon–Fri, in 15-min steps. Nothing is tracked or prompted outside these hours.
Lunch pause
12:30–13:30. Tracking and prompts stop. Lunch counts as a break: the battery is full when it ends.
Meetings
No calendar in M1. Meetings are not detected: a prompt can fire during a call. Calendar-aware prompts (Google Calendar, Outlook) move to M2.
Place
"Today I work from the office" or "from home". Default: office. Changes which breaks are offered.
Tracking: seated time and breaks
Day start
The first keyboard or mouse activity on the computer inside working hours starts the day and the seated timer, as long as Chrome is open (it can be in the background). No manual start.
Seated timer
Counts every minute of your working hours unless you are on a break. Short pauses under 5 min (reading, thinking, a locked screen while you grab something) still count as seated.
It measures desk time, a proxy for sitting.
What counts as a break
At least 5 min in a row with no activity on the computer: idle or screen locked, prompted or not. Under 5 min, it is not a break and the seated time keeps running.
Screen locked
Treated like idle. Locked under 5 min: nothing happens, the seated timer keeps running. Locked 5 min or more: a break is logged for the whole away time. On return, the battery is recharged to 100% and the seated timer restarts from 0.
Not a break
Nothing is excluded in M1. A silent call of 5+ min with no input counts as a break; accepted for the test week.
Computer asleep
Chrome open but no tick for 6+ min (lid closed): counted as away from the last tick, so 5+ min is a break.
Chrome closed
Nothing is tracked while Chrome is closed. On reopening, the gap since the last recorded activity decides:
  1. Under 5 min (update, crash, restart): counted as seated. No question.
  2. 5 min or more: the startup tab asks once, "Chrome was closed for 40 min. Did you step away?" Yes, I moved logs a break and fills the battery. No, I kept working counts the gap as seated.
  3. No answer: counted as seated. If that passes the interval, the break prompt follows.
  4. Only the part of the gap inside today's working hours, outside lunch, counts.
When unsure, count as seated: a wrong "seated" costs one early prompt; a wrong "break" hides real sitting.
Battery
Level = 100% − seated time ÷ interval. Green when full, amber at half, coral near empty. The new tab shows "Next break in mm:ss" and "N min since your last active break".
Day end
At the end of working hours: "Your day is done. 7 of 8 breaks. See how your day went." Action: See recap.
Triggers and visibility
Where it runs
In the background whenever Chrome is open, even minimised or behind other apps. Activity is detected anywhere on the computer (Slack, VS Code, Zoom…), not only in Chrome. If Chrome is closed, nothing runs; there is no "open Chrome at login" step.
Chrome opens
micro.breaks opens as the first tab, pinned, every time Chrome starts. Only one micro.breaks tab exists: if it's already open, it is reused.
Always visible
The pinned micro.breaks tab, and every new tab: the battery and countdown.
Interrupts
  1. −5 min: heads-up notification.
  2. 0 min: micro.breaks opens a new tab with the prompt and switches to it, bringing its Chrome window to the front. A system notification fires at the same time, because macOS may keep another app (Slack, Zoom) in front of Chrome. The Chrome icon also flashes.
  3. +5, +10, +15 min: reminder notifications, then silence.
Notifications stay
Every notification stays on screen until you act on it (no auto-dismiss) and carries a Start break button.
Day timeline
  1. 9:02 Chrome opens → micro.breaks is the first tab. 9:04 first activity → day starts, battery full.
  2. 9:59 heads-up. 10:04 prompt.
  3. 10:04–10:10 away → break logged, battery full.
  4. 11:05 heads-up for the next break, due 11:10 (the interval restarts when you come back, not on the clock).
  5. 12:30 lunch: silent. 13:30 battery full.
  6. 18:00 recap notification, then silent until tomorrow.
Never
Outside working hours, during lunch, and on weekends.
Health check
If notifications are blocked, the micro.breaks tab and every new tab show one line to fix it: "Notifications are off. micro.breaks can't reach you outside Chrome."
First run
On install
The micro.breaks tab opens on setup: "Let's set up micro.breaks. Two steps, about a minute."
  1. Set your movement timer: keep the defaults or edit them.
  2. Allow notifications, and make them stay: allow, set Chrome to Alerts in macOS System Settings › Notifications, send a test, confirm "Yes, it stayed".
Start moving unlocks once steps 1 and 2 are done.
Focus modes
macOS Focus / Do Not Disturb silences all notifications. The setup screen says so; the tabs still work.
Prompts and reminders
Heads-up
5 min before the break is due: a Chrome notification, "Nearly time to move. Get ready for your active break in 5 min." Action: Start break now. Once per cycle.
At zero
Together with the prompt tab: "Time to move. 1 h 00 seated. Your break is ready in Chrome." Action: Start break.
Break prompt
When the battery hits zero: a new tab opens with "Time to move." and becomes the active tab. Only one prompt tab is ever open; reminders reuse it. Start a break now on the new tab opens it early.
Start my break
Enter. Opens the break timer for the chosen break.
Remind me later
Esc. Closes the prompt and starts the reminders.
No reaction
If the prompt stays open with no answer, it behaves like "Remind me later": reminders fire at +5, +10, +15 min. The tab stays open.
Reminders
A notification every 5 min, 3 times (+5, +10, +15 min): "Your break is waiting. 1 h 05 seated." Action: Start break. After the third, reminders stop. The battery stays empty and the new tab shows the overdue time until you take a break.
Skip this one
Logged as a skip. No reminders. The next prompt comes one interval later.
After the reminders
If no break is taken, the next prompt comes one interval after the first prompt (e.g. 11:00, then 12:00).
Reminder action
Reminders are notifications only; they never open a tab on their own. Their Start break reopens the prompt tab (the same one if still open) and switches to it.
After Skip or a failed episode
No heads-up: the battery is already empty. The next prompt fires one interval after the skipped or failed prompt.
Unanswered
An episode fails 5 min after the third reminder (+20 min) if no break was logged.
While away
Once the computer reports you away (idle 5 min, idle 15 s during the break timer, or locked), nothing interrupts: prompts wait, reminders that come due are dropped (later ones still fire). A break timer waiting for you to leave does not silence reminders. The computer only reports idle 5 min after the last input, so a prompt can still open in the first minutes after you leave; the break counts when you come back.
Prompt episode
A prompt plus its reminders, until it resolves. It succeeds when a break is logged, and fails on a skip, after the third unanswered reminder, or at day end. An episode still open when lunch starts is cut and left out of the goal. This is the unit of the 80% goal.
The break (M1, before the exercise library)
Principle
No exercises yet. A break is a timer and one simple idea to get you away from the screen. The library of flagged exercises comes in M2.
Choices on the prompt
Three cards, keys 1 2 3:
  1. Energy · Take the stairs: "Up and down a floor or two, at an easy pace." At home: Walk around.
  2. Focus · Get some water: "Refill your water, look out of a window on the way."
  3. Pain relief · Stand and stretch: "Stand up, roll your shoulders, reach up, walk a few steps."
Default selection: Energy. It keeps the design's intents, with plain activities instead of routines.
Break timer
Full screen: the activity, its one-line cue and a countdown of time away from the computer. Length 5 (default), 10 or 15 min. Before you leave, the full length waits: "Leave the computer to start the timer." About 15 s after your last input it counts down; the screen says "Leave the computer. The break counts after 5 min away."
Back early
The countdown pauses while you are at the computer: "Still here? The timer continues when you leave again." It resumes from where it stopped the next time you leave. Time away adds up (3 min + 2 min = 5 min).
I'm back
Ends the timer early. If your time away adds up to 5+ min, the break is logged and "Recharged." plays. If not, nothing is logged and you return to the overdue screen.
Cancel break
Closes the timer without logging a break. The episode continues: overdue screen, remaining reminders still fire.
Validated
When the time away during the timer adds up to 5 min. The ring's 5-min mark turns green. The break is logged when you come back ("Recharged."); with 10 or 15 min, the countdown first continues to its end. A countdown never ends while you are at the computer. While the timer runs, the computer reports inactivity after 15 s instead of 5 min.
Timer ends
A notification, "Break done. Welcome back. +6 min of movement. Battery recharged." (action: Open). On return: "Recharged.", "+N min of movement" (the real away time) and "Break N of M today" (M = daily goal).
Main screen states (pinned tab and every new tab)
Before working hours
Battery full, grey, still. "BEFORE WORKING HOURS", big "9:00". "Your day starts at 9:00, at your first activity." Only the movement timer chip; no Start button.
Normal
Battery draining. "Next break in 17:50". "42 min since your last active break". Actions: Start a break now, movement timer.
Lunch
Battery fills up to green. "LUNCH · 12:30–13:30", "Lunch.", "Tracking paused until 13:30. Your battery will be full." No Start button.
Break overdue
From the prompt until a break is logged. Battery empty, coral, strong halo. Eyebrow "BREAK OVERDUE BY", big number counts up: "8:54". Line: "1 h 08 since your last active break", plus "· reminder 2 of 3 at 11:15" while reminders are running. Action: Start a break now (opens the prompt).
Overdue, reminders stopped
Same screen, line: "Reminders stopped. Take a break when you can."
After Skip
Same screen, line: "Skipped. Next prompt at 12:04."
After working hours
Battery grey, still. "FRIDAY · 18:00", "Done for today." Inline: "6 of 8 breaks · 6 h 40 at your desk · longest 72 min". Action: See your day.
Weekend or day off
Battery grey. "SATURDAY", "Weekend." "See you Monday at 9:00." Action: See Friday. The Today pill shows the last working day ("Friday · 7 of 8").
When "Recharged." appears
Break from the timer
Full "Recharged." screen on the break tab when you come back.
Prompt open, you just left
Away 5+ min without clicking: the prompt tab turns into "Recharged." on return. The episode succeeds.
Unprompted break
E.g. a 10-min coffee. No notification, no interruption. The recharge screen plays once the next time you look at the micro.breaks tab or open a new tab: "WHILE YOU WERE AWAY · Recharged. · +10 min away · Counted as break 3 of 8 today", then the normal screen.
"Yes, I moved"
After Chrome was closed: "Recharged." plays right there: "CHROME WAS CLOSED · YOU MOVED · +40 min away".
End of lunch
No animation. The battery is simply full at 13:30.
Recap
Access
A live pill in the main screen header, next to the theme toggle: "Today · 3 of 8" with one dot per goal break. Always visible, opens the recap. Until the end of working hours the page is "Your day so far" ("Recharged 3 times so far.", "5 to go before 18:00.", hours still to come shown empty, action Back to work); after, "Your day". The end-of-day notification opens the same page.
Daily goal
Breaks taken out of the goal. Default goal = working minutes minus lunch, divided by the reminder interval, rounded up. 9:00–18:00 with a 1 h lunch: 480 ÷ 60 = 8; every 45 min: 480 ÷ 45 = 10.7 → 11. 8:30–18:00 every 60 min: 510 ÷ 60 = 8.5 → 9.
Headline
"Recharged N times today."
Active breaks
N / goal, one mini battery per goal break (full when taken). "N short of your daily goal." or "Daily goal reached."
Seated time
Total seated minutes inside working hours, lunch not counted. Chart: one bar per working hour, 12 blocks of 5 min, split seated / moving / lunch (hatched).
Longest stretch
Longest run of seated minutes without a break, with its start and end ("13:59 to 15:11").
Time moving
Total minutes of logged breaks, "away from the screen".
Export
A small "Export data" text link under the recap downloads the event log and daily stats as JSON, including prompt episodes (succeeded, failed, cut) so the 80% goal can be checked.

Open decisions

None. Every M1 rule is validated and every M1 screen is designed.

Design direction

Dark-first, one big number, a battery that drains. Each screen has a single headline set very large (the countdown, "Time to move.", "Recharged.") and one black or white pill to act on. The battery is the product's metaphor: it drains from green to amber to coral while you sit, and refills when you move. Illustrations are soft 3D objects; everything else stays grey.

Source of truth: micro.breaks — M1 screens (Claude Design export v2, 32 frames, dark and light). It replaces design.md and the first export wherever they disagree.

Principles

One big number

Each screen leads with one huge figure or phrase. Everything else is 22 px or less.

The battery is the only live color

Green, amber, coral, with a matching halo behind it. The UI chrome stays grey.

Dark by default

Dark is the default; light is one tap away and remembered. Both ship on the same tokens.

Keyboard-first prompt

1 2 3 to choose, Enter to start, Esc to be reminded later. No mouse needed.

Celebrate the recharge

Breaks end with a short, joyful refill animation, then straight back to work.

Motion

  • Battery: fill height eases linearly each second; fill "breathes" (opacity 1 → .75, 3.2 s); a light shine rises through it (3.4 s).
  • Player: illustration bobs (−22 px, ±3°, 1.6 s), paused with the timer; progress segments fill live.

  • Reduced motion: every animation off; final states shown directly.

Voice

  • Short, calm, second person. Numbers lead: "42 min since your last active break".
  • Actions say what happens: "Start a break now", "Start my break", "Remind me later", "Skip this one", "Back to work".
  • Headlines are two words and a full stop: "Time to move.", "Recharged.", "Break logged."
  • Cues are physical and countable: "Big, slow circles backwards. 10 times."

Accessibility

  • Countdown and move timer use role="timer" with aria-live="off"; the battery is role="img" with "Battery at N percent until your next break".
  • Intents are a radiogroup; length options use aria-pressed; the wheel is a listbox.
  • Keyboard: 1 2 3, Enter, Esc on the prompt; ↑ ↓ on the wheel. Visible focus on every control.
  • prefers-reduced-motion turns off breathing, shine, bob and the recharge sequence.

Architecture

One rules engine holds every product rule from the PRD as a pure function: step(state, input, now) → { state, effects }. Inputs are ticks, idle changes and user actions. Effects are "show nudge", "open prompt", "log event". The background service worker feeds it and executes the effects. Every screen reads state and sends actions; no screen contains rule logic.

MV3 service workers sleep, so state is saved to storage after every step and restored on wake.

Trade-offs

Extension now, web app later

Only an extension runs without an open tab, opens itself when Chrome starts, owns the new tab and can switch you to a tab when a break is due. Sharing and accounts come with a web app in M2.

Laptop activity as a proxy for sitting

Cheap and automatic, but a standing desk or a call without typing fools it. A silent call is the worst case; calendar holds come in M2.

60-min rhythm, not 30

The evidence favours moving every 30 min. 60 min is the ceiling a busy workday will accept; tighten it once the habit holds.

Local only, event log first

No account friction for the test week. The log shape is the sync contract, so M2 adds a backend without reworking data.

A timer now, exercises later

The only thing M1 must prove is the habit of stepping away. A timer plus one simple idea needs no content work and makes validation trivial: 5 min without activity. The guided player and its library come in M2.

No calendar in M1

Google Calendar needs a Google Cloud project and an OAuth client, too much setup for a one-person test week. Prompts may fire during calls; that is accepted. Calendar-aware prompts come in M2.
